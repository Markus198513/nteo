NTEO / TECHNOLOGY

More than a print. The right material system.

The same artwork can look very different on matte, gloss, clear or perforated media. That is why we start with the application: where the graphic will be installed, viewing distance, exposure to sunlight and moisture, and how often the surface will be cleaned.

MATERIALS

Main types of self-adhesive film

Explore the main material classes and typical applications. The exact product series is selected against the manufacturer's technical specifications.

Important: these properties are not mutually exclusive. A film can be cast by manufacturing process, gloss by finish and have a permanent adhesive. Matte and gloss describe the surface finish, not the manufacturing method.
01

Calendered vinyl

A cost-effective option for short- to medium-term graphics on flat surfaces. Calendered ranges include monomeric and polymeric films, which differ in dimensional stability and recommended service life.

02

Cast vinyl

A highly conformable premium material for complex shapes, recesses and pronounced curves. Commonly specified for vehicle wraps where conformability and dimensional stability matter.

03

Matte vinyl

A low-glare finish for interior graphics, information panels and glass applications where a calm, readable appearance is preferred.

04

Gloss vinyl

A reflective finish that delivers vivid colour and visual impact. Often selected for retail windows and graphics designed to stand out.

05

Transparent vinyl

Allows the substrate to remain visible. For glass, the artwork must be planned around white ink, partial white backing or a no-white transparent effect.

06

Perforated One Way Vision

Micro-perforations allow graphics to be viewed from outside while retaining a degree of visibility from inside. Used on shopfront glazing and vehicle windows.

07

Translucent backlit film

A specialist printable film for internally illuminated displays and lightboxes. It transmits and diffuses light; the exact product is selected for the lighting and sign construction.

08

Reflective materials

A specialist optical structure that returns part of the light towards its source. Used in selected wayfinding, identification and vehicle-marking applications; it does not replace certified road-sign materials where required.

09

Fluorescent materials

High-visibility materials for accent graphics, temporary campaigns and specialist visual applications. Not all fluorescent materials are designed for wide-format printing; compatibility must be checked for the exact product.

HOW FILMS DIFFER

Choose film for the application

01

By manufacturing process

Calendered and cast films differ in how they are made, their conformability, dimensional stability and ability to follow complex shapes.

02

By optical finish

Matte, gloss and clear films create different visual results. A compatible laminate can further change the finish.

03

By application

Perforated films are designed for glazing, translucent films for illuminated displays, and reflective materials for applications that need to return light.

ADHESIVE SYSTEM

Adhesive systems and installation

The adhesive affects installation, dwell time and eventual removal. Selection depends on the substrate and operating conditions.

01

Permanent adhesive

For long-term applications. Removal may require specialist products and cannot be guaranteed residue-free on every substrate.

02

Removable adhesive

For temporary campaigns and graphics intended to be removed. Results depend on dwell time, temperature and substrate condition.

03

Easy Apply / air-release channels

The adhesive structure helps air escape during application and can make large graphics easier to install. Surface preparation and correct technique are still essential.

MATERIAL CLARITY

Self-adhesive vinyl and banner fabric are different materials

Self-adhesive vinyl has an adhesive layer and is applied to a prepared surface. Banner fabric is a flexible printable substrate, usually fixed with eyelets, pockets or a track system. For construction hoardings and large outdoor displays, the specification depends on wind load, fixing method and overall dimensions.

FINISH & PROTECTION

Protective lamination: matte or gloss

Lamination is not a separate printable film category; it is a clear layer applied over the print. It can help protect the image from abrasion and dirt while changing the finish. For outdoor use, the film, ink and laminate should be specified as a compatible system.

  • Matte — reduced glare and a subdued appearance.
  • Gloss — shine and a vivid-looking finish.
  • The choice depends on material compatibility and the application.

NTEO · FAQ

Printed film questions

What is the difference between calendered and cast vinyl?

Calendered vinyl is commonly used on flat and moderately curved surfaces for shorter-term projects. Cast vinyl is better suited to complex shapes and deeper recesses. The exact choice depends on the product series.

Does every printed film need lamination?

No. It depends on location, handling and cleaning, sun exposure, expected service life and the manufacturer's recommendations.

Can you print on clear film?

Yes. The artwork must be planned for the background it will sit on and whether a white ink layer is needed to preserve colour.

Does One Way Vision provide full visibility from inside?

No. Visibility depends on the perforation pattern, lighting on both sides, image coverage and operating conditions. The effect can change significantly at night.

How do you choose film for a vehicle?

Complex curves and full wraps are generally specified with cast systems and compatible laminates. Other film series may suit flatter areas. Vehicle shape and expected service life matter.

Important: category names describe material classes; they do not guarantee identical performance across brands. Service life, compatible substrates, installation temperature and removability must be checked against the technical data sheet for the exact product series.
